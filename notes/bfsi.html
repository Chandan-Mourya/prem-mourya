What is CASA? must

CASA = Current Account Savings Account — the deposit products customers hold for daily banking.

Now coming to types.

Savings — interest, limited transactions. Current — business, overdraft possible.

Savings account vs current account.

A savings account earns interest and has transaction limits; a current account is non-interest-bearing with unlimited transactions, suited for businesses.

Savings account

Interest-bearing (e.g., 3-4% p.a.); RBI limits free transactions per month; minimum balance required.

Target: individual retail customers; KYC mandatory; overdraft typically not available.

Current account

No interest; unlimited transactions; overdraft facility available; higher minimum balance.

Target: businesses, traders, firms; multiple signatories; cheque book mandatory.

Now coming to conclusion.

In core banking, both are CASA products but parameterized differently — product code drives the interest and limit rules.

What is customer onboarding?

Customer onboarding is the end-to-end process of registering a new customer: collecting personal details, completing KYC, risk assessment, and opening the first product account.

Steps

Collect personal details: name, DOB, address, PAN, Aadhaar — create CIF (Customer Information File).

KYC verification: document collection, identity verification (in-person or eKYC via Aadhaar OTP), risk category assignment.

Account opening: select product, set account parameters, generate account number, issue debit card/kit.

Digital onboarding

Video KYC (V-KYC): RBI-approved remote KYC via live video — used in digital banking onboarding.

Integration points: CKYC registry, CIBIL check, PAN validation, NSDL Aadhaar verification.

Now coming to conclusion.

Incomplete KYC blocks account operations — regulatory requirement under PMLA and RBI KYC directions.

What is AML?

AML (Anti-Money Laundering) is a set of regulatory controls and monitoring processes to detect, prevent, and report suspicious financial transactions that may indicate money laundering.

Controls

Transaction monitoring: rule-based and ML-based alerts on unusual patterns (large cash deposits, structuring, high-risk geography transfers).

Customer due diligence (CDD): ongoing risk assessment based on customer profile, transaction behavior.

Reporting

Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) filed with FIU-IND within 7 days of suspicion.

Cash Transaction Report (CTR): mandatory for cash transactions above ₹10 lakh.

Technical

AML engines (Actimize, Mantas) integrate with core banking via real-time or near-real-time feeds.

Every flagged transaction is investigated; analyst clears or escalates; audit trail maintained.

Now coming to conclusion.

AML compliance failures attract massive regulatory fines — coding AML integration correctly is non-negotiable.

What is CIF/customer ID?

A CIF (Customer Information File) is the master customer record in core banking — a unique ID linking all accounts, relationships, and interactions for one customer.

Structure

CIF holds: name, DOB, address, contact, PAN, Aadhaar, KYC status, risk category, relationship manager.

One CIF → multiple accounts (savings, current, FD, loan) — all linked by customer ID.

Banking importance

Single customer view: relationship manager sees all products held by a customer across CIF.

KYC status at CIF level controls account operations — if CIF KYC expires, all accounts restricted.

Now coming to conclusion.

CIF deduplication is critical — multiple CIFs for same customer cause compliance and service quality issues.

What is account opening process?

Account opening in core banking involves CIF creation or linking, product selection, parameter setup, account number generation, and regulatory checks.

Steps

Create or find existing CIF; select account product (savings, current, OD); enter account-specific parameters.

Run deduplication check (avoid duplicate accounts for same customer); check negative list / blacklist.

Maker-checker approval; generate account number; link debit card, cheque book; send welcome kit.

Regulatory

PAN linkage mandatory; Aadhaar seeding required; nominee registration; FATCA/CRS declaration for applicable customers.

Now coming to conclusion.

Account opening triggers a CIF snapshot for audit — all data at opening time is preserved for regulatory review.

What is transaction posting?

Transaction posting records a financial event in core banking: validating the transaction, debiting/crediting accounts, updating balances, and creating the audit trail.

Process

Validate: account active, sufficient available balance, daily/transaction limit, AML screening.

Post debit and credit entries maintaining double-entry balance; update account balance atomically.

Generate reference number, audit log entry, advices/receipts, and notification events.

Atomicity

Posting is atomic — either both debit and credit post, or neither does. Partial postings cause reconciliation failures.

Oracle ACID transactions ensure posting integrity; compensating transactions reverse erroneous postings.

Now coming to conclusion.

Every posted transaction must balance: sum of all debits = sum of all credits in the GL.

What is debit and credit in banking?

In double-entry banking accounting, debit increases asset/expense accounts and decreases liability/equity accounts; credit is the opposite.

Accounting perspective

Customer savings account is a liability for the bank (bank owes customer) — credit increases it, debit decreases it.

Loan account is an asset for the bank — debit increases it (disbursement), credit decreases it (repayment).

Customer perspective

Customer sees debit as money out of account (withdrawal, payment); credit as money in (deposit, receipt).

Every transaction has equal debit and credit — Golden Rule of Double Entry maintained in GL.

Now coming to conclusion.

Understanding accounting polarity is essential for writing correct posting logic in core banking systems.

What is ledger balance vs available balance? must

Ledger balance = official book balance. Available balance = what customer can spend now (ledger minus holds/liens).

Now coming to example.

Cheque deposited but not cleared — ledger may credit, available may wait.

What is hold amount/lien amount?

A hold amount temporarily blocks funds in an account (e.g., for a cheque clearing); a lien is a legal claim on funds (e.g., for a loan collateral or court order).

Hold

Placed for: clearing cheques, pending debit authorizations, disputed transactions — released automatically on clearance or expiry.

Available balance = Ledger balance - Hold amount; customer cannot withdraw held funds.

Lien

Placed for: collateral against loan, court attachment, tax demand — requires formal release by authority.

Lien is more permanent than a hold; blocks both withdrawal and any further operations on liened amount.

Now coming to conclusion.

Always check available balance (after holds and liens) before authorizing debit transactions.

What is interest calculation?

Interest calculation in banking applies a rate to a principal amount over a period: simple interest (I = P*R*T) or compound interest, accrued daily and credited periodically.

Savings account

Daily accrual method: interest accrued on daily closing balance; credited monthly or quarterly to account.

Accrual PL/SQL runs in EOD batch: for each account, calculate (balance * rate / 365) and accumulate in interest_accrual table.

Loans

EMI uses reducing balance method: each month interest on outstanding principal; balance reduces with each EMI.

NPA classification: if account overdue > 90 days, stop accruing interest; reverse already-accrued income.

Now coming to conclusion.

Interest calculations must use NUMBER precision types — never FLOAT — to avoid rounding errors in financial amounts.

What is GL account?

A GL (General Ledger) account is the master accounting book entry that records all financial transactions of a bank — organized by chart of accounts.

Structure

GL accounts are classified: Assets (loans, cash, investments), Liabilities (deposits, borrowings), Income (interest, fees), Expense (interest paid, opex).

Every customer transaction posts to GL: customer account debit → corresponding GL credit, or vice versa.

Banking operations

EOD GL balancing: sum of all debit GL entries = sum of all credit GL entries across the bank — any mismatch triggers exception.

MIS reports aggregate GL data; regulatory reports (balance sheet, P&L) generated from GL.

Now coming to conclusion.

GL integrity is the financial backbone — any posting error must be reversed with proper authorization and audit trail.

What is reconciliation?

Reconciliation is comparing two independent records of the same financial data to detect and resolve discrepancies — e.g., bank's internal records vs. RBI/SWIFT statements.

Types

Nostro reconciliation: bank's internal records of foreign currency account vs. correspondent bank's statement.

Payment reconciliation: transactions sent to NEFT/RTGS vs. confirmed settlements received from RBI.

GL-to-subledger reconciliation: GL account balances match sum of individual customer account balances.

Technical approach

Load external statement to staging table; join with internal transaction table on reference number; identify unmatched rows.

Categories: matched, unmatched in internal, unmatched in external — each needs investigation and resolution.

Now coming to conclusion.

Automated reconciliation with Oracle MERGE statements identifies breaks in seconds across millions of records.

What is EOD/BOD process?

EOD (End of Day) closes the business day with interest accrual, GL consolidation, and reporting; BOD (Beginning of Day) opens the next day and executes standing instructions.

EOD steps

Date change validation → interest accrual → charge posting → GL netting → MIS report generation → statement trigger.

Each step logged; failure halts subsequent steps; ops team resolves and re-runs from failure point.

BOD steps

Open new business date; process standing instructions (auto-debit, recurring transfers); mature FDs; release expired holds.

SLA

EOD must complete by a defined time (e.g., 2 AM) to allow BOD and branch opening next morning.

Now coming to conclusion.

EOD is the highest-risk scheduled process — monitor with real-time alerts and have a documented recovery procedure.

What is batch processing in banking?

Batch processing executes high-volume, time-deferred banking operations (interest accrual, EMI collection, statement generation) in scheduled jobs without real-time user interaction.

Characteristics

Processes millions of records efficiently using PL/SQL BULK COLLECT + FORALL or Java parallel streams.

Scheduled via DBMS_SCHEDULER (Oracle) or Quartz/Spring Batch (Java); runs during off-peak hours.

Banking batch types

Daily: EOD/BOD interest accrual, charge posting, payment settlement confirmation.

Monthly: statement generation, EMI due calculation, fee collection, MIS reports.

Periodic: regulatory reports (RBI returns), NPA classification, provisioning.

Now coming to conclusion.

Batch failures must be resumable from the point of failure — never re-run the entire batch from scratch on a large table.

NEFT — batch settlement, no min amount. RTGS — real-time high value (typically ≥ ₹2 lakh). IMPS — instant 24x7 including mobile.

What is SWIFT?

SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) is the global messaging network for interbank financial transactions using standardized message formats (MT/MX).

How it works

Banks send SWIFT messages (MT103 for credit transfer, MT202 for bank-to-bank, MT940 for account statement) via secure SWIFT network.

Messages identified by BIC (Bank Identifier Code); routed through SWIFT FIN or SWIFT gpi network.

Message types

MT103: customer credit transfer (international wire). MT202: financial institution transfer. MT950: statement message.

MX (ISO 20022): XML-based next-gen format; SWIFT migrating all traffic to MX by 2025.

Integration

Banks connect via SWIFT Alliance or third-party SWIFT service bureau; messages ingested by OSB and processed by core banking.

Now coming to conclusion.

SWIFT MT → MX migration is a major ongoing project in banking IT — understanding both formats is valuable.

What is loan lifecycle?

The loan lifecycle covers origination, credit appraisal, disbursement, repayment scheduling, EMI collection, and closure (or NPA classification if defaulted).

Stages

Origination: application, document collection, credit score check, approval workflow (maker-checker).

Disbursement: loan account created, limit set, amount credited to customer CASA account.

Repayment: EMI auto-debit on due date; prepayment, part-payment handling; penal interest on delay.

End states

Closure: full repayment, account closed, lien released, NOC issued.

NPA: 90+ days overdue triggers NPA classification; interest accrual stopped; provisioning created.

Now coming to conclusion.

Loan lifecycle involves complex state machine logic — correctly implementing state transitions is critical for NPA and regulatory compliance.

What is EMI?

EMI (Equated Monthly Installment) is a fixed payment per month comprising principal and interest calculated using the reducing balance method.

Calculation

EMI = P * r * (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1) where P = principal, r = monthly interest rate, n = tenure in months.

Each EMI: interest portion = outstanding_principal * monthly_rate; principal portion = EMI - interest.

Core banking

EMI schedule generated at disbursement and stored in installment_schedule table.

Auto-debit on due date from CASA account; if insufficient balance, mark installment as overdue and apply penal interest.

Now coming to conclusion.

EMI calculations must use precise NUMBER types and be verified against regulatory loan disclosure requirements.

What is NPA?

NPA (Non-Performing Asset) is a loan where the borrower has not made interest or principal payments for 90 days or more — classified under RBI guidelines.

Classification

Sub-standard: NPA for up to 12 months. Doubtful: NPA for 12-36 months. Loss: irrecoverable — write-off recommended.

NPA classification triggers: stop interest accrual, reverse income already booked, create provisioning.

Technical

Automated NPA classification runs in EOD batch: identifies accounts with overdue days > 90 and updates NPA flag.

Provision calculation: regulatory percentages (15% sub-standard, 100% loss) applied to outstanding balance.

Now coming to conclusion.

NPA management is heavily scrutinized by RBI — automated, accurate classification is a compliance requirement.

What is overdraft?

An overdraft allows a customer to withdraw more than their account balance up to a sanctioned overdraft limit, essentially a revolving credit against an asset.

Types

OD against FD: bank allows overdraft up to 80-90% of FD value at FD rate + 1-2%; FD is lien-marked as collateral.

OD against salary: pre-approved based on salary credits; popular in retail banking.

Core banking

Account balance can go negative up to -OD_LIMIT; interest charged on utilized OD amount daily.

OD limit stored as account parameter; available balance = OD_LIMIT - |negative balance|.

Now coming to conclusion.

Overdraft interest calculation logic must correctly handle the negative balance scenario in the accrual procedure.

What is standing instruction?

A standing instruction (SI) is a pre-authorized recurring payment instruction from a customer — executed automatically by the system on specified dates without manual intervention.

Types

Intra-bank transfer: periodic transfer between own accounts (savings to RD, loan EMI auto-debit).

Inter-bank transfer: NEFT/RTGS to another bank account on fixed date (utility bills, SIP).

Processing

BOD batch processes all SIs due for the day: checks sufficient balance, executes transfer, logs result.

On failure (insufficient balance): SI marked failed, notification sent to customer, retry may be configured.

Now coming to conclusion.

Failed standing instructions should generate alerts and follow-up actions — critical for loan EMI SIs.

What is cheque clearing?

Cheque clearing is the process by which a cheque deposited in one bank is presented to the drawer's bank for payment via the clearing house (NPCI CTS in India).

Process

Payee deposits cheque at their bank (collecting bank); cheque image captured and sent to CTS (Cheque Truncation System).

Drawee bank verifies signature, balance, stop payment; if OK, authorizes payment; funds transferred via settlement.

Hold impact

Collecting bank places hold on the cheque amount until clearing confirmation — typically 1-2 business days.

On clearance: hold released, funds available; on dishonor: hold removed, debit reversed, dishonor charges applied.

Now coming to conclusion.

CTS (NPCI) handles ~70 lakh cheques/day in India — understanding the lifecycle is important for exception handling in banking IT.

What is payment gateway?

A payment gateway securely captures and routes payment information from customer to acquiring bank and payment network, returning authorization responses in real time.

Flow

Customer submits payment → gateway encrypts card/UPI data → routes to acquiring bank → card network (Visa/RuPay) → issuing bank → authorization.

Response (approved/declined) returned to merchant within seconds; settlement occurs separately.

Banking integration

Banks integrate payment gateways via APIs (REST/SOAP); transaction data logged for reconciliation.

Chargeback process: disputed transaction reversed through card network; bank must maintain records for dispute resolution.

Now coming to conclusion.

Payment gateway integration must handle timeout, partial auth, and retry idempotently — failed auth must never double-debit.

Why audit trail is important in banking?

Audit trails are legally mandated in banking to detect fraud, support regulatory investigation, ensure accountability, and enable transaction reversal with full history.

Regulatory

RBI, SEBI, and PMLA regulations require banks to maintain transaction records for 5-10 years.

Regulators can demand audit logs during inspection — incomplete trails attract penalties.

Operational

Fraud investigation: trace every action on a customer account — who accessed, what was changed, when.

Dispute resolution: customer disputes a debit — audit trail provides exact timestamp, channel, and approver.

Technical

Append-only audit table with hash chaining detects tampering; triggered by Oracle AFTER DML triggers.

Now coming to conclusion.

Audit trails must be immutable — no UPDATE or DELETE should ever touch audit log tables.

What is product customization?

Product customization in core banking is code-level modification of the vendor product (FLEXCUBE) to implement bank-specific business logic not available through configuration.

Types

Hook-based: FLEXCUBE provides hook points (pre/post processing hooks) where custom PL/SQL procedures are plugged in.

Gateway customization: custom validation, enrichment, or format transformation at interface gateway layer.

Risks

Customizations increase upgrade complexity — every vendor patch must be tested against custom code.

Minimize customization: use configuration first; customize only when product cannot meet the requirement.

Now coming to conclusion.

Each customization must be documented with business justification — helps during vendor-upgrade impact analysis.

What is product configuration?

Product configuration is setting up banking product behavior through parameters and rules within the product's admin screens — without changing any code.

Examples

Configuring a savings account product: interest rate, minimum balance, free transaction limit, charge schedule.

Configuring a loan product: interest rate type (fixed/floating), repayment method, penal interest rate, prepayment charges.

Advantages

No code change means no regression risk and no impact on vendor support.

Business team can configure new products/variants without IT involvement — faster time-to-market.

Now coming to conclusion.

Configuration is always preferred over customization — products like FLEXCUBE are 80% configurable.

Configuration vs customization.

Configuration uses product parameters and admin screens (no code change); customization modifies or extends product code for requirements not met by configuration.

Configuration

Setting product parameters, charges, interest rates, workflow rules via product admin screens.

Safe for vendor upgrades; supported by vendor; business teams can often do it themselves.

Customization

Writing hook procedures, custom validations, new screens, or modifying existing reports.

Risky for upgrades; must be re-tested with every patch; increases maintenance cost.

Now coming to conclusion.

Rule: exhaust configuration options first; only customize when business requirement cannot be met any other way.

What is parameterization in banking product?

Parameterization is defining the system-level settings (holidays, currencies, business date, charge codes) that control the product's behavior across all modules.

Examples

Business date parameter: controls current working date for all transactions and batch processing.

Holiday calendar: defines non-working days; affects value dating, interest calculation, and SI execution.

Charge codes: defines fee types (cheque return charge, SMS alert fee) with amount and posting GL account.

Now coming to conclusion.

Incorrect parameterization (wrong business date, missing holiday) causes widespread incorrect processing — validate carefully in SIT.

What is interface development?

Interface development builds the integration layer between core banking and external systems — using APIs, messaging, or file-based exchange to transfer data reliably.

Types

Real-time: REST/SOAP API calls between systems for immediate response (balance inquiry, fund transfer).

Batch/file: fixed-format or XML files exchanged periodically (RBI return files, SWIFT statements).

Event-driven: JMS/Kafka events published by core banking consumed by downstream systems.

Banking examples

NEFT/RTGS interface: constructs ISO 20022 XML from transaction data; sends to RBI payment system; processes settlement confirmation.

CBS-to-CRM interface: publishes customer and account events via JMS; CRM consumes to keep data in sync.

Now coming to conclusion.

Every interface must handle error scenarios: retry logic, DLQ monitoring, and reconciliation to detect missed messages.

What are inbound and outbound interfaces?

Inbound interfaces receive data into core banking from external systems; outbound interfaces send data from core banking to external systems.

Inbound

Payment confirmations from RBI NEFT/RTGS: core banking receives settlement status and updates account.

KYC data from CKYC registry: customer verification data pulled or received by core banking.

Outbound

Transaction advices to SMS/email gateway: notifications sent after account transactions.

RBI regulatory returns: formatted files sent to RBI (SLR, CRR, NPA data) on regulatory schedule.

Now coming to conclusion.

Both directions need error handling, logging, and reconciliation — undelivered outbound messages are as dangerous as missing inbound.

What is report customization?

Report customization is modifying standard product reports or creating new reports to meet bank-specific data requirements, formats, or regulatory specifications.

Types

Modification: add columns, change layout, filter criteria on existing standard FLEXCUBE/Jasper report.

New report: build from scratch using Oracle BI Publisher, JasperReports, or PLSQL data extraction + Excel template.

Process

Define data requirements with business; write optimized query (avoid full scans on large transaction tables).

Test with realistic data volume — a report that runs in 10 seconds on 1M rows may timeout on 100M rows.

Now coming to conclusion.

Always index the report query's WHERE clause columns — untuned reports are the #1 cause of database performance degradation.

How do you handle bank-specific business rules?

How do you handle go-live support?

Go-live support involves war room presence, real-time monitoring, rapid incident response, and direct support to end-users during the critical first days of a new banking system going live.

Pre-go-live

Cutover checklist: data migration validation, environment health check, rollback plan confirmation, support team briefing.

Dry run: simulate full cutover in staging environment to discover issues before production.

Post-go-live

Hypercare: dedicated support team on-site or on-call 24/7 for first 2-4 weeks.

Monitor: application logs, DB performance, batch completion times, error rates — act on any anomaly immediately.

Now coming to conclusion.

Go-live day is not the end — hypercare period determines whether the project is truly successful.

How do you handle production defects after go-live?

Post-go-live production defects are triaged by severity, communicated to business, fixed with root cause analysis, and deployed via emergency change management process.

Triage

Sev1 (core banking down): immediate hotfix, all hands on deck; communicate ETA every 30 minutes.

Sev2/3 (data or functional issue): workaround if possible; fix in next patch cycle within agreed SLA.

Fix process

Root cause analysis → fix developed in DEV → tested in SIT/UAT → emergency change request raised → production deployment.

All hotfixes must go through a fast-track but not zero-test process — banking cannot afford blind deployments.

Now coming to conclusion.

Document every post-go-live defect with RCA — patterns reveal gaps in testing that should inform future projects.

What is regression testing in product implementation?

Regression testing verifies that new patches, customizations, or configuration changes have not broken existing working functionality.

Approach

Maintain a regression test suite covering all core business flows: account opening, transaction posting, interest accrual, EOD batch.

Automate where possible: Selenium for UI, REST Assured for APIs, DBUnit for data layer — run on every deployment.

Banking specifics

After every patch: run full regression suite on SIT; focus on areas listed in patch notes as changed.

Financial calculations regression: compare pre/post patch interest accrual results on same dataset.

Now coming to conclusion.

Regression test suite is an investment — the more comprehensive it is, the safer each deployment becomes.

Environment setup provisions all servers, databases, middleware, and application software for a specific testing or production tier with environment-specific configuration.

Environments

DEV: developer workstations or shared dev server; frequent deployments, minimal data.

What is SIT/UAT/Production environment?

SIT validates integrated components; UAT validates business scenarios with bank users; Production serves live customers under strict change control.

Environments

SIT: IT-driven integration testing; frequent deploys; synthetic or subset data; external systems may be stubbed.

UAT: business users execute real scenarios; anonymized production-like data; sign-off gate before go-live.

Production: HA/DR, 24x7 monitoring, real money and audit obligations — no direct testing here.

Banking promotion

Artifacts promote DEV → SIT → UAT → PROD with evidence at each gate.
